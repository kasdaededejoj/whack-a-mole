## Verdict

The guide captures the recorded language and preserves uncertainty, but it is stronger on atmosphere and governance than on the intended visitor experience. The highest-value next decision is what the first meaningful discovery should make a visitor feel and understand. **8/10 — design judgment, not user-tested.**

## Clarify

1. **Experiential promise.** Define the first discovery's emotional/intellectual consequence before choosing a setting. Curiosity leading to recognition and a personal connection is one proposed arc; it is not approved. Mystery, unease, welcome, and appreciation are different choices.
2. **Meaning of the archetype in use.** Define how normality, mysticism, and unconventionalism influence choices. Do not require three literal symbols or all three in every scene. A familiar form, an unexplained relation, and an unexpected transformation are possible interpretations only.
3. **How much the visitor can understand.** Distinguish uncertainty about meaning/destination from uncertainty about controls or whether an action registered. Decide whether "no explanations" concerns lore, instruction, controls, or all three before using it as a universal design constraint. Preserve the recorded principle while making its scope explicit.
4. **Angular form versus flow.** A visual study should show how low-poly geometry, oversized proportions, movement, light, and color coexist. Record the result rather than assuming either angularity or flowing forms must dominate.
